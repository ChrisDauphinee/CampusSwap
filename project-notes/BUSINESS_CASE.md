# Business case

Your prototype shows *what* the product looks like. This document explains *why* it could become a business. Answer in your own words and refer to course concepts by name. See the term project brief on Moodle for length and grading.

## 1. The idea

- **Customer:** Who exactly is it for?
- **Problem:** What problem do they have today, and how do they solve it now?
- **Solution:** What does your product do differently or better?
- **Revenue:** How does the company make money?

CampusSwap is an online marketplace designed specifically for university students. The main target customers would initially be students attending StFX. University can be expensive, especially when students have to pay for tuition, textbooks, furniture and other everyday items. Currently, students use online marketplace, group chats and word of mouth to buy and sell used products. The problem with these options is that it can take time to find what they are looking for, and sometimes they have to travel outside their university community to pick something up. CampusSwap would make this process easier by allowing students attending the same university to buy and sell products in one place. Students could search for specific items, filter through categories, save products and create their own listings. This could help students save both time and money. The website would be free to use, but CampusSawp would make money by allowing sellers to pay a small fee to promote their listings. This would give their products more exposure and potentially help sell them faster. I would start the business at StFX before expanding to other universities because it would be easier to manage and understand one market at a time.

## 2. Where IT creates value

Which parts of the value chain does your product change, and how? Which course concepts apply (e.g. scale, distribution channels, differentiation, data, switching costs)?

Information technology creates value for CampusSwao by making the process of buying and selling products easier and more efficient. Two main parts of the value chain that would benefit from this are marketing and sales and customer service. Instead of students having to advertise their products in multiple places, they could create one listing on CampusSwap. Buyers could then use the search bar and category filters to find what they need. This would reduce the amount of time students spend looking for products. CampusSwap sets itself apart because it focuses specifically on university students instead of trying to serve everyone. This would help separate it from other online marketplaces. Another benefit of information technology would be the ability to collect data. CampusSwap could use information about popular searches and listings to understand what students are looking for. For example, textbooks might be more popular at the beginning of a semester, while furniture might be more popular near the end of the school year. Campusswap could also benefit from economies of scale. As more students join, the cost of operating the website per user could decrease because the same technology can support many different users.

## 3. Network effects 
Does your product get more valuable as more people use it?

- Is it a **one-sided** or **two-sided** market? Who are the sides?
- Where does the extra value come from as the network grows?
- How would you solve the "empty network" problem at launch?
- Point to the part of your prototype that shows this.

CampusSwap would benefit from two-sided network effects because there are two main groups using the website, buyers and sellers. Both groups benefit when more people from the other group join. When more sellers join CampusSwap, buyers have more products to choose from. This makes the website more valuable to buyers because they have a better chance of finding what they need. When more buyers join, sellers have a better chance of selling their products. This could encourage even more sellers to join. One of the biggest challenges when starting CampusSwap would be the empty network problem. If there are no products listed, students will have no reason to visit the website. At the same time, sellers will not want to list products if there are no buyers. To solve this problem, CampusSwap would start at StFX instead of launching at several universities at once. The business could advertise through student groups, campus events and word of mouth. Offering free promoted listings at the beginning could also encourage students to start selling products. Starting near the beginning or end of the school year could help atrract users because these are times when students may be looking to buy or sell university essentials. The network demo section of my prototype demonstrates this concept. Users can change the number of buyers and sellers using two sliders and see how the number of available listings and potential buying opportunities increases. However, the network effects would mainly be local. having many users at another university would not really help someone attending StFX. CampusSwap would need to build an active network at each university before expanding further.

## 4. Can it be copied? (the moat question)

Anyone in this class could rebuild your website in a weekend with the same AI tool. So the code is **not** your competitive advantage.

- What about your business is **valuable, rare, hard to imitate and well-organized** (VRIN)?
- What would a competitor with a bigger budget need to copy you, and how long would it take?
- Which of your advantages get *stronger* over time (data, network, brand, switching costs)?

The CampusSwap website itself would not be difficult to copy. Since AI can create websites quickly, another person could build a similar website without having much coding experience. This means the website itself would not be my main competitive edge. Instead, campusSwap would need to develop advantages that are harder for competitors to copy. These could be evaluated using the VRIN framework, which looks at whether resources are valuable, rare, difficult to imitate and non-substitutable. The first potential advantage would be an established community of university students. This would be valuable because having more active buyers and sellers makes the website more useful. It could also be rare if CampusSwap became the main marketplace used by students at a particular university. Although a competitor could copy the website, it would take time to attract the same number of active users. However, the advantage would not be completely non-substitutable because students could still use other online marketplaces. Students could also use multiple marketplaces at the same time. Another potential advantage would be relationships with universities and student organizations. These relationships would be valuable because they could help campusSwap reach more students without spending as much money on advertising. They could also become rare if CampusSwap developed strong relationships that other marketplaces did not have. These relationships would take time to develop, making them somewhat difficult to imitate. However, other companies could still build their own relationships or use paid advertising instead, meaning this advantage would not meet every part of the VRIN framework. A third potential advantage would be the reputation and information CampusSwap collects over time. As students complete transactions, they could build a reputation through reviews. This would help buyers decide who they can trust. campusSwap could also collect information about which products students are interested in. This would be valuable because it could help the business make better decisions. Historical information could be difficult for a new competitor to recreate right away, although competitors could collect their own information over time. If a competitor had a much larger budget, they could probably create a similar website within a weekend. They could also spend more money on advertising and offer incentives to attract students. However, building an active student community and developing trust could take several months or longer. CampusSwap would also need to be well organized by properly managing its customer information, university relationships and customer service. Overall, CampusSwap would not have a guranteed competitive advantage. Its strongest advantages would be its student community, reputation and relationships. These could become stronger over time as more students use and trust the website. 


## 5. Cloud and operations

What would it take to run this for real? Which parts would you buy as cloud services (SaaS, PaaS, IaaS), and what are the risks of relying on them?

For CampusSwap to become a real business, it would need several features that are not included in the prototype. These include real student accounts, a shared database, payment processing, messaging and security. CampusSwap could use software as a service (Saas) for student login systems and customer support. This would allow the business to use existing software instead of having to build everything itself. Platform as a service (PaaS) could be used to host the website and manage the database. This would make it easier to store information and allow students to access the same listings. Infrastructure as a service (IaaS) could be used to rent servers and storage if CampusSwap eventually needed more control over its technology. However, this would probably not be necessary when first starting the business. One risk of relying on cloud services is that the website could stop working if a provider experiences any outages. Another risk is that providers could increase their prices, making the business more expensive to operate. campusSwap could reduce these risks by regularly backing up information, monitoring expenses and making sure information can be transferred to another provider if needed. The additional features, estimated costs and difficulty of creating a real version are explained in my project log.


What could go wrong — for your customers, their data, or society? What would you do about it?

One of the biggest risks campusSwap would face is scams and fake listings. Someone could advertise a product they do not actually own or lie about its condition. CampusSwap could reduce the risk by verifying student accounts and allowing users to report suspicious listings. Another risk would be student privacy. The website could contain personal information such as email addresses and private messages. CampusSwao would need to protect this information and only collect what is necessary for the business to operate. Student safety would be important because buyers and sellers may have to meet in person. CamousSwao could encourage students to meet in public areas on campus instead of sharing their home addresses. The website would also need to clearly identify promoted listings so students understand when someone has paid to advertise their product. CampusSwap could have a positive impact on the environment by encouraging students to reuse products instead of throwing them away.
